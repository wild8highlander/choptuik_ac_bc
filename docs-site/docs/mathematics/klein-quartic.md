# Klein Quartic Curve

The **Klein quartic curve** is the central geometric object in the Choptuik AC/BC framework. It is a smooth projective curve of genus 3 that attains the Hurwitz upper bound on the number of automorphisms, making it one of the most symmetric algebraic curves in existence.

## Definition

The Klein quartic is the projective plane curve defined by the homogeneous polynomial

\[
\mathcal{K} : x^3 y + y^3 z + z^3 x = 0 \quad \subset \mathbb{CP}^2.
\]

In affine coordinates \(z = 1\), this becomes the affine equation

\[
x^3 y + y^3 + x = 0.
\]

The curve is smooth: the partial derivatives \(\partial \mathcal{K} / \partial x\), \(\partial \mathcal{K} / \partial y\), and \(\partial \mathcal{K} / \partial z\) have no common zero on \(\mathcal{K}\), which can be verified by direct computation.

## Genus and the Hurwitz Bound

The Klein quartic has **genus** \(g = 3\). The **Hurwitz bound** states that a compact Riemann surface of genus \(g \geq 2\) has at most \(84(g - 1)\) automorphisms. For genus 3, this gives an upper bound of 168. The Klein quartic **attains this bound**, making it a **Hurwitz curve**—one of only finitely many curves to achieve the maximum symmetry.

\[
|\mathrm{Aut}(\mathcal{K})| = 168 = 84(g - 1).
\]

## Automorphism Group

The full automorphism group is isomorphic to the **simple group of order 168**:

\[
\mathrm{Aut}(\mathcal{K}) \cong \mathrm{PSL}(2, 7) = \mathrm{SL}(2, 7) / \{\pm I\}.
\]

This group is the second-smallest non-abelian simple group (after \(A_5 \cong \mathrm{PSL}(2, 5)\)). It can be presented as

\[
\mathrm{PSL}(2, 7) = \langle\, a, b \mid a^2 = b^3 = (ab)^7 = [a, b^4 a b^{-4}] = 1 \,\rangle.
\]

### Generators as Projective Transformations

The automorphisms are realized as linear transformations of \(\mathbb{CP}^2\). Two generators are:

\[
\alpha : [x : y : z] \mapsto [\zeta x : \zeta^2 y : z], \qquad \zeta = e^{2\pi i / 7},
\]

\[
\beta : [x : y : z] \mapsto [y : z : x],
\]

where \(\alpha\) has order 7 and \(\beta\) has order 3. The full group is generated by these two elements together with an involution.

## Hessian and Syzygetic Relations

The **Hessian** of the Klein quartic—the determinant of the matrix of second partial derivatives—is a polynomial of degree 6 that vanishes on a curve of degree 6. The Klein quartic and its Hessian satisfy a remarkable syzygetic relation: the Hessian curve intersects \(\mathcal{K}\) transversally in 24 points, which correspond to the **inflection points** (Weierstrass points) of the curve.

## Connection to the Choptuik Framework

The Klein quartic provides the geometric foundation for the entire Choptuik AC/BC package:

1. **Spinor phases** \(\varphi_k\) are derived from the action of \(\mathrm{PSL}(2,7)\) on the cohomology group \(H^0(\mathcal{K}, K_{\mathcal{K}})\).
2. The **Dirac operator** is constructed using the Poincaré metric on \(\mathcal{K}\) and the spin structure induced by the canonical bundle.
3. The **Choptuik critical exponent** \(\gamma\) is expressed as a spectral invariant of the Dirac operator on \(\mathcal{K}\).

!!! note "Historical Remark"
    The Klein quartic was first studied by Felix Klein in 1879 in his work *"Über die Transformation siebenter Ordnung der elliptischen Funktionen"*. Klein recognized its exceptional symmetry and its connection to the group \(\mathrm{PSL}(2,7)\), which he called the "group of the seventh-order transformation."
